\begin{resumo}[Abstract]
 \begin{otherlanguage*}{english}
Machine learning models in production degrade over time due to data drift,
a change in the data distribution that silently compromises the validity of
a trained model. While detecting this phenomenon is a relatively mature
problem, deciding how to respond to a detected drift remains rarely
investigated systematically. This work evaluates, through a controlled
factorial experiment in a stream learning setting, the effectiveness of
four retraining strategies (full reset, sliding window, incremental
retraining, and historical model reactivation) and a no-retraining control,
under four temporal drift patterns, three severity levels, and three
classifiers (Naive Bayes, Random Forest, and XGBoost), in 180 conditions and
1,800 runs. Severity is operationalized through the Hellinger distance,
exact for the generating distributions and estimated by the adopted
detector (HDDDM). Drift type, severity, and classifier all interacted
significantly with the strategy, but what most distinguished the strategies
was how they are triggered: the sliding window was the most effective
strategy in 29 of the 36 combinations, with a large advantage under gradual
and incremental drift, where the detector signals the change in the middle
of the transition and detection-triggered strategies retrain too early, at
the cost of about four times as many retraining runs. Full reset was the
best strategy under high-severity abrupt drift, reactivation matched full
reset at a lower cost, and the effectiveness of incremental retraining
depended on the classifier. As an exploratory extension, the detector's
observable signals estimated the drift's severity (Spearman correlation of
0.90), speed (AUC of 0.97), and recurrence well; a guideline built from them
outperformed always applying full reset, but not a fixed policy of always
using the sliding window.
   \vspace{\onelineskip}

\noindent \textbf{Key-words}: data drift. concept drift. stream learning.
model retraining. drift detection.
 \end{otherlanguage*}
\end{resumo}
